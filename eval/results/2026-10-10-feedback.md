`274f1f6`). Reviewer `claude-haiku-4-5`, judge and reflection model `sonnet` (Claude Code
2.1.296). Test split: 5 invented sessions × 3 repeats = 15 reviews per run. Every call
goes through the product's `runClaude`, so its 100 s timeout applies.

## 2. GEPA search

`optimize --metric-calls 60 --max-model-calls 200`: 89 calls (48 generate, 36 judge,
5 reflect), 297,092 input tokens (35,640 cached), 281,299 output, $2.08.

It stopped early. 12 reviewer calls hit the 100 s timeout and tripped the pilot's error
cap, so GEPA returned the seed. Since then, the runner scores a timeout as a failed review and
does not count it as an error (the product hits the same limit and retries later). Its best
candidate by mean score on what it saw, `ea9d2f71` (0.767 over 6 trials, seed 0.573 over 15),
is in `2026-10-10-feedback-gepa-candidate.diff`. The candidate defines each area more precisely,
tells the reviewer not to speculate about what the agent did, and asks for every later fact
to be carried back.

One hand-written sentence was also tried ("You see only the developer's prompts, never what
the agent did: ..."): 7/15, mean 0.563, 3 timeouts, 5 `grounded` failures; 27 calls,
62,141 / 11,880 / 88,654 tokens, $0.57. No gain. **Not shipped.**

## What this does and does not show

- The gap review beats the old rubric on every session and every shared measure.
- The same shipped wording scored 0.581 in run 1 and 0.718 in run 3. With 15 reviews per run,
  only large differences mean anything.
- `claude-haiku-4-5` ran past 100 s on 1 to 3 of 15 shipped reviews under parallel load. In
  the product that delays feedback and does not lose it. It is the cost that limits any
  longer prompt.
- Most remaining `grounded` failures come from the session with no follow-ups
  (`no-followups-vague`). The judge rejects any claim that the agent "had to guess" when nothing
  in the session shows it. Whether a review should name gaps there at all is a product
  question, and it is still open.
